# LunarRescue — conditional product concept

**Prepared September 21, 2026. Not a completed NASA-data demo or a verified 2026 challenge submission.** Challenge-specific wording, duration and slide count remain pending the full statement and current submission guide. Existing v0.2.0 predates the hackathon.

## Problem

An apparently short route can demand more time or effort across uneven terrain. Learners need a clear way to connect terrain, limited resources and mission priorities.

## Target users

Learners and public science-event visitors interested in planetary exploration. The product is an educational game, not professional astronaut training.

## Solution

The existing Windows prototype supports Moon and Mars rescue missions. The proposed next stage adds one verified lunar region, two route choices, a transparent gameplay budget and an evidence-based mission debrief.

## How NASA data would be used

A candidate LRO/LOLA elevation grid would drive terrain elevation and derived route profiles. Metadata, processing steps and limitations would ship locally. The current release does not contain this terrain integration.

## What players would learn

Compare distance with terrain; weigh optional science against the remaining budget; compare a plan with observed results; distinguish measured and interpolated data from authored objects and gameplay rules.

## What is innovative

The proposed contribution is the connection between a player's route decision, a traceable planetary dataset and a debrief grounded in that session. This is a design intention, not a validated novelty or learning-outcome claim.

## Limitations

Current terrain is authored. LOLA grid spacing does not resolve small rocks or establish astronaut safety. Suit resources and movement are simplified. Dataset import, planner, session telemetry and educational debrief are not implemented. Eligibility and reuse of existing work are unconfirmed.

## Next steps

Confirm challenge and reuse rules; verify the dataset and a traversable region; implement and test the complete loop during an authorized development window; run new-user playtests.

## Conditional demo sequence

1. State the route/resource decision problem.
2. Show the actual imported region alongside product ID, resolution and sources; do this only once integration exists.
3. Compare and confirm two measured routes.
4. Show observed time/resource use during rescue and the optional decision.
5. Compare plan with actual outcome and explain one measured lesson.

Until those features exist, demonstrate v0.2.0 separately and label all future screens as concepts. Do not substitute a fictional terrain screenshot for a NASA-derived region. Timing and slide count will follow verified 2026 requirements rather than earlier-year limits.
