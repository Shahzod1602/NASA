# Field-training playtest protocol

Status: prepared only. Human participants and human results: **0**.

Ask the same three questions before and after play:

1. Is the shortest route always the best choice when terrain and a shared battery
   matter? Explain one reason.
2. Does an inspected cartridge at Apollo 12 prove water was measured at that site?
   Identify the remote evidence source and the authored teaching prop.
3. When battery, cargo mass and local data storage are limited, what could you
   postpone, deposit or transmit, and what does the decision cost?

Give a new player the clean packaged build without coaching. Ask them to select
Apollo 12, complete EVA checks, preview a difficulty budget, find and inspect the
training rover, drive a short distance, exit, inspect and pack a cartridge, then
return and deposit it. Let them choose whether to complete optional support work
before continuing the primary historical objectives. Repeat a short rescue test
for labelled cable matching when time permits.

Record version/executable SHA, hardware/settings, accessibility scale, completion,
time, wrong inputs, confusing wording, camera/exit issues and comments in the
participant's own words. Avoid personal identifiers. Record observer help and
incomplete attempts. Do not derive learning improvement until actual before/after
answers exist; automated fixtures are not human playtests.
